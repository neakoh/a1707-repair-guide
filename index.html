Keyboard shell (top case) transplant: MacBook Pro 15" Touch Bar (A1707)

This guide covers moving the logic board, display and Touch ID button of a MacBook Pro 15" Touch Bar (2016–2017, A1707) into a replacement top case.

The keyboard is riveted into the top case. It can be replaced on its own, but that means cutting out every rivet and screwing the new one in; swapping the whole top case is the easier route, and the same swap fixes a cracked palm rest or a liquid-damaged case. A used top case with the battery, trackpad, speakers, fans, battery board and microphone already fitted costs about £60 on eBay.

iFixit covers the battery, screen, speakers and trackpad for this model but not the top case, and nothing covers moving Touch ID: the button is paired to the logic board and only works if it moves with it. This guide puts the whole job in one place, with a screw map for every step.

Expect glued ribbons that need warming and a Touch ID cable that must not crease. About 3–4 hours.

You need

  • This toolkit has every bit and tool below except the hair dryer (no affiliation)
  • P5 pentalobe (bit ★1.2)
  • P2 pentalobe (bit ★0.8)
  • T3 Torx
  • T5 Torx
  • T8 Torx (bit T8H)
  • Plastic spudger
  • Blunt tweezers
  • Opening picks
  • Suction cup
  • Hair dryer (not a heat gun)
  • A magnetic project mat for the screws (recommended), or half sheets of A4 and a pen
  • Keep every screw: as each step takes screws out, put them in their own labelled spot, marked with that step's number. They go back in reverse, from the same spots.
  • Touch ID only works with the original logic board. A torn ribbon kills it permanently.
  • Battery power connector off before touching anything else.
  • Never mix screws: several sizes differ by 0.1 mm.

Taking it apart

Disable Auto Boot

This MacBook powers on when the lid opens. In Terminal, before shutting down:

sudo nvram AutoBoot=%00

Turn it back on in the last step.

Why: This laptop switches itself on when the lid opens or power is connected. The lid opens and closes many times during the repair, and the battery is reconnected near the end. With Auto Boot on, the board can power up while a connector is in hand or a metal screwdriver is near it. That can short and kill the board. Run the command on the laptop being repaired.

Taking it apart

Drain the battery and power off

Run the battery below 25%, then shut down completely and unplug the charger.

Why: A full battery holds much more energy. If a screwdriver slips and punctures or shorts a cell, a low cell is far less likely to overheat or catch fire.

Done when: Below 25%, powered off, charger unplugged.

Taking it apart

Check the new shell has every part fitted

This guide is for a replacement top case that comes with all six of these already fitted:

  • Both fans
  • The battery (all six cells), stuck down
  • Both speakers (the boxes with grilles at the hinge corners)
  • The trackpad, with its ribbon cable
  • The battery board (the small board at the battery's edge, with the data cable)
  • The microphone (a short ribbon ending beside a fan, with a black pull tab)

Check every one is there before starting, and that the battery cells are flat, with no swelling at the corners. If any part is missing, this guide doesn't cover moving it across.

Why: Parts that sit under other parts must go in first. Finding a missing part at the end means taking everything out again.

⚠A missing part found at the end costs a full teardown.

Done when: All six parts fitted, and the cells are flat.

Taking it apart

Set up a place for the screws

A magnetic project mat is best: screws stay where they're put, and the surface can be written on. Without one, use half sheets of A4, one per step that takes screws out.

Each time a step takes screws out, mark a spot on the mat or a half sheet with the step number and the screw lengths, and put the screws on it. Steps with two lengths get two marked spots.

Why: Many screws look the same but differ by a fraction of a millimetre. A screw that is too long can drill through the board underneath. The marked spots show where each one came from; work back through them in reverse to rebuild.

⚠Several sizes differ by 0.1–0.2 mm. A long screw in a short hole punches through the board.

Done when: Mat or half sheets and a pen beside the laptop.

Taking it apart

Remove the six lower-case screws

Close the lid and flip the laptop over. Remove the six P5 pentalobe screws (bit ★1.2). Note the angle: they go back in at a slight tilt.

Comes out · onto the spot marked step

4× P5 4.7 mmlower case
2× P5 6.6 mmlower case, hinge end

Taking it apart

Lift a gap with the suction cup

Press the suction cup down near the front edge, between the screw holes. Pull up just enough to open a slot.

Why: The lower case has no edge to grip. The suction cup lifts it just enough for a pick to get in, without metal tools that scratch the aluminium.

Taking it apart

Release the two side clips

Slide an opening pick into the gap, then around the nearest corner and halfway up the side until the clip pops. Repeat on the other side.

Why: Hidden clips hold the case along the sides. Pulled off before they release, they bend or break and the case won't sit flat again.

Taking it apart

Slide the lower case off toward you

Get fingertips under the front edge and pull firmly away from the hinge, one corner at a time. If it sticks at the hinge, lever it from the hinge cutout near the headphone jack with the flat end of the spudger.

Why: The clips at the hinge end are hooks. They release when the case slides, and snap off when it lifts.

⚠Pull sideways, not up. Lifting snaps the hinge-end clips.

Done when: Lower case off; the inside is visible.

Taking it apart

Peel off the battery board cover

The large rectangular cover on the board edge nearest the battery. Warm it with the hair dryer if it resists.

Why: The battery's cables are under this cover. Disconnect the battery before touching anything else.

Taking it apart

Disconnect the battery data cable

Peel back the tape, flip up the ZIF locking flap with the spudger, then slide the cable out parallel to the board.

Why: This is the first half of cutting the battery off from the board, so nothing has power during the work.

Taking it apart

Disconnect the battery power connector

Remove the pancake screw, then lift the connector with the spudger.

Why: This cuts the power completely. Every connector unplugged after this is safe to touch. Skip it, and unplugging a live connector can short and damage the board.

⚠Lift it high enough that it can't drop back onto its socket. If it touches during the work, it can destroy the logic board.

Comes out · onto the spot marked step

1× T5 3.7 mm pancakebattery connector

Done when: Connector held clear of its socket.

Taking it apart

Disconnect the trackpad cable

Remove the cover bracket, then pry the connector straight up.

Why: The trackpad must come out before the logic board can lift clear, and its cable holds it to the board.

Comes out · onto the spot marked step

2× T3 1.9 mmtrackpad cable bracket

Taking it apart

Free the trackpad ribbon from the old battery

Warm the ribbon with the hair dryer until it's warm to the touch, then peel it up slowly and push it aside.

Why: The ribbon is glued down. Warm glue lets go; cold glue tears the ribbon.

⚠If it resists, add heat, not force. Don't fold it.

Taking it apart

Remove the thirteen trackpad screws

Two lengths that look identical. Keep them in separate piles.

Why: Two lengths look the same. The longer screws in the wrong holes push through into the trackpad mechanism.

Comes out · onto the spot marked step

9× T5 5.8 mmtrackpad
4× T5 4.9 mmtrackpad

Taking it apart

Drop the trackpad out and catch the washers

Keep the laptop upside down and swing the lid open slightly. The trackpad drops onto the display. Feed its ribbon out through the chassis slot.

Why: The washers set the trackpad's height. Without all nine, the trackpad sits crooked or does not click properly.

⚠Nine tiny washers sit loose on the posts. Count all nine before moving on.

Comes out · onto the spot marked step

9× Trackpad washerscrew posts

Done when: Trackpad out, nine washers accounted for.

Taking it apart

Disconnect the keyboard

Remove the cover bracket and pry the connector straight up.

Why: The keyboard is part of the old top case. Its cable must come off the board before the board can move.

Comes out · onto the spot marked step

2× T3 1.9 mmkeyboard bracket

Taking it apart

Disconnect the display board flex

Remove the flex cover, then the connector bracket, then pry the flex straight up.

Why: The display connects to the board here. The screen stays on the old case until later, so this cable must be free first.

Comes out · onto the spot marked step

2× T3 3.5 mmdisplay flex cover
2× T3 1.7 mmdisplay connector bracket

Taking it apart

Remove both hinge covers

Two screws per hinge.

Why: The covers hide the hinge screws and some connectors. Nothing near the hinges is reachable until they are off.

Comes out · onto the spot marked step

4× T3 2.0 mmhinge covers

Taking it apart

Remove the Touch ID / headphone bracket

One bracket covers both connectors. Remove it, then pry the headphone jack connector straight up.

Why: The bracket holds the Touch ID and headphone connectors down. They cannot come up while it is in place.

Comes out · onto the spot marked step

2× T3 2.4 mmTouch ID / headphone bracket

Taking it apart

Disconnect Touch ID

Pry the power button / Touch ID connector straight up off the board. No rocking, no sideways pressure.

Why: The Touch ID button stays with the old case until the transfer. Its cable must come off the board first, gently, because it cannot be replaced.

⚠This cable can't be replaced. From now on, never let it take any tension.

Taking it apart

Disconnect the Touch Bar digitizer

Remove the single screw. With tweezers, slide the bracket toward the side edge until it clears its tab, then pry the cable straight up.

Why: The Touch Bar is part of the top case. Its cables must come off before the board can lift out.

Comes out · onto the spot marked step

1× T3 1.3 mmdigitizer bracket

Taking it apart

Disconnect the Touch Bar display

Remove the bracket, pry the connector straight up.

Why: This is the Touch Bar's second cable. Both must be free.

Comes out · onto the spot marked step

2× T3 1.9 mmTouch Bar display bracket

Taking it apart

Disconnect both Thunderbolt flexes

Remove the cover screws (two per side). Pry each flex up from the inside edge, nearest the fan, and push it aside.

Why: The USB-C ports are separate boards joined to the logic board by these cables.

Comes out · onto the spot marked step

4× T3 1.3 mmThunderbolt covers, 2 per side

Taking it apart

Disconnect both speakers

Slide the flat end of the spudger under each cable near its connector and twist gently.

Why: The speakers are fitted in the new shell, so the old ones stay behind. Their cables must come off the board.

Taking it apart

Disconnect the microphone

Peel the tape, flip up the ZIF flap, then pull the cable toward the fan by its tape.

Why: Same reason: the cable runs from the old case to the board and must be free before the board moves.

Taking it apart

Disconnect the three antenna cables

Three thin black antenna wires (Wi-Fi/Bluetooth) run down from the hinge, beside the right-hand fan in the photos, and plug into the logic board. A small clip holds them, fixed by one screw.

  1. Remove the clip's single screw, where the heat pipe meets the fan.
  2. Each wire ends in a tiny round gold plug, like a miniature press stud. Slide the flat end of the spudger under the wire up to the plug and twist gently: it pops straight up. Repeat for all three.

The wires stay attached to the display; they only need to be off the board.

Why: The antennas sit in the display hinge area. The board cannot lift away while they are attached.

⚠Lever under the plug, never pull the wire itself.

Comes out · onto the spot marked step

1× T5 2.9 mmantenna bundle

Done when: All three plugs free. That's the last of the board's 14 cables: nothing should still be plugged in.

Taking it apart

Remove the ten logic board screws

Four lengths across three drivers. This is the worst place to mix screws up.

Why: With every cable free, these screws are all that hold the board in.

Comes out · onto the spot marked step

2× T3 2.8 mmlogic board
5× T5 2.9 mmlogic board
1× T5 3.7 mmlogic board
1× T8 3.9 mmlogic board
1× T8 3.8 mmlogic board

Taking it apart

Lift the fan dampers

Peel the two rubber vibration strips up off the fans, but leave them attached.

Why: The rubber strips overlap the board edge. Left stuck down, they catch the board.

Taking it apart

Lift the logic board out

Lever the left edge up with the spudger and lift the board away from the left.

Why: The board moves into the new case, and must be out before the display hinges can be reached.

⚠It should come out with no resistance. If it snags, stop and find the cable still connected.

Done when: Board out, Touch ID flex still attached to the old case and unstrained.

Taking it apart

Remove the display board

Four screws. When it goes back, the display flex must sit on top of the board, not under it.

Why: This small board connects the screen to the logic board. It moves to the new case with the screen.

Comes out · onto the spot marked step

4× T5 3.3 mmdisplay board

Taking it apart

Remove the antenna assembly screws

Sixteen tiny P2 screws (bit ★0.8).

Why: The antenna assembly covers the hinge screws. These tiny screws hold it.

Comes out · onto the spot marked step

16× P2 1.5 mmantenna assembly

Taking it apart

Lift the antenna assembly off

The antenna assembly is one long bar across the whole hinge edge, hinge to hinge. iFixit's photos show only one end close up.

  1. Start at one end, by a hinge. Slide the pick under the bar and lift straight up.
  2. Work along the bar a little at a time, lifting each time. Do not slide the pick along under it.
  3. Take extra care in the middle: the display ribbons pass under the bar there.
  4. Lift the bar away with its cable bundle. With the logic board out, there is no heat sink to feed the bundle under.

If it will not lift, a screw is still in. Count 16 P2 screws for step 31, and check both ends by the hinge brackets. No adhesive is used here; if all 16 are out and it still grips, a minute of hair dryer on low along the bar is safe.

Why: With it off, the hinges can be reached to free the screen.

⚠Never slide the pick sideways under the bar: the display ribbons under the middle tear.

Done when: The whole bar is out in one piece with its cable bundle.

Taking it apart

Remove the display cable springs

Four screws hold the two springs over the display cables.

Why: The springs hold the display cables down at the hinge. They must come off before the screen can separate.

Comes out · onto the spot marked step

4× T3 3.9 mmdisplay cable springs

Taking it apart

Remove two screws from each hinge

Leave the third screw in each hinge for now.

Why: The third screw keeps the screen attached until the last step, so it doesn't fall.

Comes out · onto the spot marked step

4× T8 4.9 mmhinges

Taking it apart

Separate the display

Tape the glass above each hinge. Open the lid fully, stand the machine on its side, and remove the last screw from each hinge, topmost last. Push the halves together to free the hinges, then pull them apart.

Why: The screen moves to the new case. It must come off the old one first.

⚠Either half can fall without warning. Keep a hand on both.

Comes out · onto the spot marked step

2× T8 4.9 mmhinges, last pair

Taking it apart

Remove the fan over the Touch ID corner (old case)

In the old case, the fan on the Touch ID side sits over the button's backplate. It has to come out first. The other fan stays.

  1. Take out every screw on this fan's rim (about three; Torx T5).
  2. Its cable came free with the logic board. Lift the fan straight out.

Source: Vinship's A1707 video, 4:56 to 5:17, does this before the Touch ID work.

Why: The backplate screws are under the fan.

⚠If T5 sits loose in a head, stop and try T3. A rounded head here is hard to get out.

Comes out · onto the spot marked step

3× T5 fan screwsfan on the Touch ID side (old case)

Done when: The fan is out and the Touch ID corner is clear.

Taking it apart

Touch ID: remove the backplate

The button sits in the corner by the hinge, at the end of the Touch Bar. A small backplate holds it in.

  1. Check the new shell's Touch ID recess for a small round silver disc where the button's bottom rests. Note whether it is there.
  2. On the old case, remove the backplate's six T3 screws. Four silver ones are in the corners; two are black. Keep the colours apart.
  3. Lift the backplate off.

Source: LaptopScreen.com's A1707 top case video, 7:36 (watch).

Why: Touch ID only works with the original logic board, so the original button must move.

⚠From here on, never let the button's flex cable take any tension.

Comes out · onto the spot marked step

4× T3 silver (corners)Touch ID backplate
2× T3 blackTouch ID backplate

Done when: Backplate off, six screws in the tray (4 silver, 2 black).

Taking it apart

Free the Touch ID button and its cable extension

Both the button's cable and the extension sit on a light adhesive strip. Warm them with the hair dryer on low for 20–30 seconds, then slide a pick in flat from the side and work it along under the cable. Lift with the pick, never by pulling the ribbon.

  1. Peel back the black tape over the power button's cable extension.
  2. Gently unfold the flex cable to the left.
  3. Under it, flip up the connector's locking lever with a plastic pick, and pull the button's connector out of the extension.
  4. Thread the button's cable back through its slot. The button then drops out the bottom of the case. It is not glued.
  5. Carefully pry off the cable extension and set it aside. It moves to the new case too.
  6. If the new shell had no round disc, lift the old one out with fine metal tweezers (light adhesive) and press it into the new recess.

Source: same video, 7:47 to 8:15 (watch).

⚠Pull only on the connector and the button body, never on the ribbon.

Done when: Button and cable extension both out, ribbon intact; the new shell has its disc.

Putting it back together · Screen and board

Fit Touch ID into the new case

  1. Make sure the round disc is in the new recess.
  2. Thread the button's cable through its slot from underneath, and seat the button.
  3. Stick the cable extension in place.
  4. With tweezers, push the button's connector into the extension. It may need repositioning to go fully in.
  5. Lock the connector lever down with a plastic pick.
  6. Press the extension and the button's cable down onto their adhesive, and fold the tape back over.
  7. Refit the backplate: silver screws in the corners, the two black screws in the other holes.

Source: same video, 8:25 to 9:08 (watch).

Done when: Button flush and clicking, connector locked, backplate on with all six screws.

Putting it back together · Screen and board

Fit the display to the new shell

Stand the new shell on its side, open like the old one was. Lower the display's hinges into their recesses and hold both halves together.

Fit all six T8 4.9 mm hinge screws loosely, three per hinge.

Why: Loose first, so the screen can be centred before anything is tightened.

⚠Either half can fall. Keep a hand on both until the screws are in.

Done when: Six hinge screws in, loose.

Putting it back together · Screen and board

Centre the screen and tighten the hinges

Close the lid and check the gap is even down both sides. Adjust, then tighten all six hinge screws.

If the screen clicks or snaps when opening later, it sits too far forward: loosen, realign, tighten.

Done when: Gaps even both sides, six screws tight.

Putting it back together · Screen and board

Refit the display cable springs

Lay the two springs over the display cables at the hinge and fit the four T3 3.9 mm screws.

Done when: Both springs on, four screws in.

Putting it back together · Screen and board

Fit the antenna assembly

Lay the long antenna bar along the hinge edge, hinge to hinge, with its three wires coming off the middle. Keep the wires out of the board area for now. Fit the sixteen P2 1.5 mm screws (bit ★0.8).

⚠These are tiny: make sure the bit is fully home before turning.

Done when: Bar flat along its whole length, 16 screws in.

Putting it back together · Screen and board

Fit the display board

Place the display board and fit its four T5 3.3 mm screws.

The display flex must sit on top of the board, never under it.

Done when: Board seated, four screws in, flex on top.

Putting it back together · Screen and board

Lower the logic board in

Before the board goes in, check every cable that will plug into it lies outside the board's area, folded back toward the case edges. None may end up underneath:

  • Antenna bundle (three thin wires) and the display board flex, at the hinge edge
  • Touch ID and the headphone jack, in the hinge corner on the Touch ID side
  • Touch Bar display and Touch Bar digitizer cables, at the other hinge corner
  • Both Thunderbolt flexes, along each side edge
  • Both fan cables and the microphone ribbon, beside the fans
  • Both speaker cables, at the speaker boxes
  • The keyboard, trackpad and battery data cables, along the battery's edge

Then:

  1. Check the two rubber dampers on the board edge sit flat.
  2. Hold the board by its edges. Gently pinch the antenna bundle together and guide it under the heat pipe while lowering the board. Use a tool to help it through; never force it.
  3. Lower it right edge first, then the left, into its place between the fans.
  4. Look round every edge: each cable from the list should be visible, not under the board.

⚠Never force it down. If it rocks, a cable is under it.

Done when: Board flat and every cable from the list visible, none underneath.

Putting it back together · Connect and test

Fit four logic board screws to hold it

Test the machine before screwing anything down for good. Connect every cable, power it on and check everything works (the test is the last step of this part). Only then do the brackets and the other screws go in.

For that test, four screws hold the board, so a connector can still be reseated easily if something fails:

  • T8 3.9 mm (left, by the hinge) and T8 3.8 mm (right, by the hinge)
  • T3 2.8 mm ×2 (the two near the battery)

Nudge the board until the connectors line up with their sockets, then tighten these four snugly.

Why: The board can't shift during connecting and testing, and little has to be undone if something fails.

⚠A long screw in a short hole punches through the board. Take each from its own compartment.

Done when: Four screws in, board can't move, connectors line up.

Putting it back together · Connect and test

Press the fan dampers down

Press the two rubber vibration strips back onto the fans.

Done when: Both strips stuck down.

Putting it back together · Connect and test

Connect both fans

The new shell's fans each have a short cable. Plug each into its socket on the board beside that fan and press it fully home.

iFixit's guides and the videos never show these sockets up close, so the diagram marks the area, not the exact socket: follow each fan's own cable to the socket it reaches.

Why: Without them the machine overheats and throttles.

Done when: Both fan cables seated.

Putting it back together · Connect and test

Connect the three antenna cables

Line each gold plug up squarely over its socket and press straight down until it clicks. Leave the bundle clip off for now: it goes back after the test.

Done when: Connector pressed flat, its whole length level.

Putting it back together · Connect and test

Connect the microphone

Flip the ZIF lever up, slide the cable in by its tape tab, flip the lever down. Re-stick the tape over the socket.

Done when: Cable fully in, lever down.

Putting it back together · Connect and test

Connect both speakers

Press each speaker connector straight down onto its socket.

Done when: Both clicked in.

Putting it back together · Connect and test

Connect both Thunderbolt flexes

Press each flex connector straight down, then fit its cover now: T3 1.3 mm ×4, two each side.

Why now: unlike the other connectors, these flexes are stiff and can spring up off their sockets without the cover holding them. iFixit doesn't say whether the test needs them; screwing them down now is the safe side.

Done when: Both seated, four cover screws in.

Putting it back together · Connect and test

Connect the Touch Bar display

Press the connector straight down until it clicks. Leave its bracket off for now: the brackets go back after the test.

Done when: Connector pressed flat, its whole length level.

Putting it back together · Connect and test

Connect the Touch Bar digitizer

Press the cable straight down until it clicks. Leave its bracket off for now: the brackets go back after the test.

Done when: Connector pressed flat, its whole length level.

Putting it back together · Connect and test

Connect Touch ID and the headphone jack

Press the Touch ID connector straight down onto the board, then the headphone jack connector. Leave their shared bracket off for now: it goes back after the test.

⚠Straight down only on Touch ID. No rocking, no tension on the cable.

Done when: Connector pressed flat, its whole length level.

Putting it back together · Connect and test

Connect the display board flex

Press the flex straight down onto its connector until it clicks. Leave its bracket and cover off for now: they go back after the test.

Done when: Connector pressed flat, its whole length level.

Putting it back together · Connect and test

Connect the keyboard

Press the keyboard connector straight down until it clicks. Leave its bracket off for now: the brackets go back after the test.

Done when: Connector pressed flat, its whole length level.

Putting it back together · Connect and test

Connect the trackpad

The new shell's trackpad cable runs across the battery. Press its connector straight down onto the board until it clicks. Leave its bracket off for now: the brackets go back after the test.

Done when: Connector pressed flat, its whole length level.

Putting it back together · Connect and test

Connect the battery data cable

Flip the ZIF lever up, slide the battery board's data cable in parallel to the board, flip the lever down. Re-stick its tape.

Done when: Cable fully in, lever down.

Putting it back together · Connect and test

Connect the battery power: last

Lower the battery power connector onto its socket and fit the T5 3.7 mm pancake screw. Then press the battery board cover back on (rotate it 180° if it won't sit flat).

Why: Power goes on only when every other connector is seated.

Done when: Connector screwed down, cover on.

Putting it back together · Connect and test

Test everything before closing

Look first: every connector from this part pressed flat; no loose screw or washer in the case; no cable trapped. The brackets are still off, so don't press on the board.

Power on: dry, non-metal surface, charger in, press the Touch ID button (Auto Boot is off). Don't touch the board while it's on.

Putting it back together · Finish

Close up and restore Auto Boot

Hook the hinge-end clips first, slide toward the hinge, press the clips home, then refit the six P5 screws at the slight angle they came out at: the 6.6 mm pair on the hinge edge. In Terminal:

sudo nvram AutoBoot=%03

If anything misbehaves, reset the SMC before assuming damage.

Why: Auto Boot makes the laptop start when the lid opens. Turn it back on so it behaves normally.
